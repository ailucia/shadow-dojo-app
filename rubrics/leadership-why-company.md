# "Why our company?"

> **Category:** Leadership & Drive · **Type:** "Why us?" / "Why do you want to work here?" / "Why are you a good fit?" · [All rubrics](README.md)

**In one line:** Don't answer the literal question ("why do *you* want this?"). Answer the one
underneath: **"What do you know about us, and how can you help?"** Tell a short story that
connects a specific thing you learned about them to what you can specifically do for them, with
scope and numbers.

---

## Why it matters
It shows up in nearly every interview. Answered naively, it exposes you as unprepared, too
junior, or both. At some companies it's a gate:
- **Anthropic:** candidates commonly fail the *recruiter screen* on "why Anthropic" beyond "I'm
  interested in AI." Read Dario Amodei's essays and the Core Views on AI Safety, and show you care
  about safety as a real problem.
- **Apple:** expect "Why Apple?" in *every round*, answered with genuine emotion and real product
  knowledge.
- **OpenAI:** strategy answers that don't connect to the AGI mission feel thin, and the same goes
  for "why OpenAI."

## Three levels of answer
- **Ugly, all about me:** "I've always been passionate about products with big impact, just like
  your mission." It answers the literal question and adds nothing to how they value you.
- **Bad, generic praise:** "your cutting-edge tech, driven culture, impressive team… my
  background fits." It *tells* rather than *shows*, with no proof of homework and no scope or
  metrics.
- **Good, a story that unites you and them:**
  1. **Specific homework:** a research paper, eng blog post, product move, or public statement
     ("I read your piece on inferring long-term effects from short-term experiments").
  2. **The need it implies:** "so you need people who are strong at X to keep leading here."
  3. **Your matching expertise, with scope and numbers:** "I lead a team doing X; it produced $Y."
  4. **Fit:** a short close.

## Traps
- Echoing the mission statement.
- "I'm passionate about…" as the whole answer.
- Praise with no evidence.
- No numbers.
- An answer you could give to any company.

## Prep per company
One specific artifact (paper, blog, launch, earnings remark) → the need it reveals → your most
relevant story or number → one line of fit. Refresh it with a live search before each loop.
