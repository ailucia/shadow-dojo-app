# Growth questions

> **Category:** Product Strategy · **Type:** growth strategy ("10x X," "grow Y by 3x," "win share," multi-year growth plan) · [All rubrics](README.md)

**In one line:** Write the goal as a simple equation so the levers are visible. Find the most
constrained lever, pick the *one* growth loop that best moves it, choose initiatives that
compound, and close with the risk, metrics for each stage, and a counter-metric that protects
what already works.

---

## The framework (4 steps)

**1. Choose the lever.** Write the goal as a simple equation. There's no single growth formula;
it depends on what you're growing:
- DAU = MAU × (average active days per user ÷ days in the period)
- Subscription revenue = paying subscribers × average revenue per subscriber
- Marketplace volume = active buyers × purchase frequency × average order value

You don't need to model the whole business. The goal is to expose the levers and find the most
constrained one. **Confirm the model with the interviewer.** Then gather context: why users show
up and stay, what's been tried, and what the company is strong or constrained at right now.

> **Retention rule:** if retention is strong, the constraint is probably acquisition or
> distribution. If it's weak, fixing it almost always beats acquiring users who won't stay.

Leave this step agreed with the interviewer on *which* lever and *why*.

**2. Choose the growth loop.** Pick the one with the strongest fit (several may help):

| Loop | Why it wins | Pick it if… |
|---|---|---|
| **Acquisition** | Direct, measurable paths to new users, fast | Awareness is the real bottleneck; big segments aren't reached yet; the product is under-penetrated |
| **Activation** | Compounds, and cheaper than acquisition at scale | Users churn before reaching value; time-to-value is too long; AI could personalize onboarding |
| **Retention** | Highest long-term ROI, hard to copy | Churn is the core constraint; long-term users behave differently; switching costs are low |
| **Community & distribution** | Most durable; compounds without ongoing spend; network effects | Power users would advocate; there's a creator dynamic; organic sharing already happens |

**3. Generate and prioritize initiatives.** Brainstorm several that feed the chosen loop, then
compare them on **impact on the lever**, **durability** (compounding or one-time), **feasibility**
given the company's strengths, and **strategic fit** (does it make future growth easier or
harder?). Recommend one, or a small set that reinforce each other.

**4. Name the tradeoffs and metrics.** State the main risk, metrics for each stage of the
journey (leading signal first, but don't mistake it for success), and a **counter-metric** that
tells you if the strategy is hurting the base.

## Traps
- Listing tactics with no equation, so you can't rank or size anything.
- Pouring effort into acquisition when retention is weak.
- Picking every loop instead of one.
- One-time wins presented as strategy.
- Treating a leading signal (invites) as success.
- No counter-metric protecting the base.
