# "Tell me about yourself" (the personal pitch)

> **Category:** Leadership & Drive · **Type:** personal pitch: "tell me about yourself," "walk me through your background," "walk me through your resume" · [All rubrics](README.md)

**In one line:** Asked in every interview, it's where the interviewer decides whether you're
worth their time, and first impressions form in seconds (presence and delivery, not just words).
Open with your level, give a short, dense "menu" of your strongest work with scale, leave
breadcrumbs toward what you *want* to be asked, tailor it to this company, and get ahead of "what
are you looking for?"

---

## Why they ask
Interviewers are busy, and most candidates are weak, so this tells them early whether the
interview is worth it. Research on thin slices found that observers predicted offer outcomes from
just the first few seconds of an introduction, *even on mute.* The pitch is your first impression.

## Don'ts (each is a small signal toward a downlevel)
- **Too thin:** they'll ask about anything.
- **A monologue:** too long, and they get bored.
- **"My name is…"** as the opener: sounds junior and scripted.
- **A laundry list of tools:** a pitch isn't a job description. At most say "I've used your
  stack for X years."
- **The origin story** ("I got into this because…"): skip it, or keep it to one sentence if
  you're breaking in.
- **Hype words:** "coolest" → "cool." Measured beats sensational.

## Do's
- **Open with your level and role:** "I'm a senior PM…" sets the anchor before any story.
- **Treat it as the menu for the meal.** The rest of the interview may run on follow-ups to
  whatever you mention, so **leave breadcrumbs** that make them curious in the direction you want.
- **Lean, dense project descriptions,** or buckets of work.
- **Add scale and impact:** users, revenue, the hard technical part.
- **Take one memorable chance,** especially with a non-traditional background.
- **Tailor it** so the details wouldn't make sense if said to a different company.
- **Get ahead of the obvious questions** ("what are you looking for next," "why us"), even
  partially.
- **End with an invitation:** "Happy to go deeper on any of these."

## Shape (PM version)
1. Level + current or most recent role + the one-line scope.
2. The flagship result, with scale, plus the hardest part (a breadcrumb).
3. The previous role's best result, with scale.
4. One line of breadth (types of work, mentoring).
5. What you're looking for next, tied to this company.
6. The invitation.

## Traps
- Starting with your name or education.
- A chronological resume walk.
- Tool lists.
- No numbers.
- Generic wording that could go to any company.
- Talking past about 90 seconds without a check-in.
