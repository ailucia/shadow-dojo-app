# Go-to-market (GTM) questions

> **Category:** Product Strategy · **Type:** go-to-market / launch · [All rubrics](README.md)

**In one line:** Don't open with channels. Figure out who feels the pain most, what they do
today, and why they'd switch now. Then pick the GTM motion that fits, sequence the launch as a
series of increasingly public tests, and measure whether people actually adopt it.

---

## The framework (4 steps)

**1. Clarify the context.** What does the product help someone do? What stage is it at
(pre-launch, early traction, or ready to scale)? Which outcome matters, over what period? What
constraints apply (budget, geography, regulation, existing customers)? A good clarifying
question changes the strategy: *"Is the goal initial traction or defensible scale? A community
motion that takes 12 months to compound won't help if we need revenue in 90 days."* Test: would
a different answer change what I recommend?

**2. Map the landscape:**
- **Who feels the pain most?** Define them by situation and need, not demographics. "Marketing
  teams at Series B startups that hand-compile weekly reports" is useful. "Businesses that need
  productivity tools" isn't.
- **What do they do today?** The real competitor is often a workaround: a junior analyst and a
  pile of browser tabs.
- **Why would they switch now?** A tech improvement, a behavior change, a policy shift, or a
  market event.

**3. Choose the GTM motion:**

| Motion | Fits when | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Product-led (PLG)** | Built-in viral loop, no org buy-in needed, shareable output | Figma, Notion, Linear |
| **Community-led** | Early adopters care deeply and are trusted by the people they refer. Slow but durable. | Cursor, Perplexity |
| **Content-led** | New category or steep learning curve; customers need education before they convert. Compounds over time. | Tutorials, comparisons, "made with X" showcases |
| **Sales-led** | Multiple stakeholders or a contract big enough to justify sales | Enterprise AI; Stripe (developer self-serve, then enterprise sales) |
| **Ecosystem / API-led** | Lives inside a bigger platform | Plugins, marketplaces, Copilot ecosystem |

**Paid acquisition is a headwind for new AI tools:** it's expensive and noisy. Early on,
organic, earned, and ecosystem distribution return more. **Sequence the motions:** start with
the one that solves today's bottleneck, and let each phase set up the next.

**4. Sequence the launch and define success.**
- **Private beta:** a small group of high-fit users. Where do they get stuck? Do they reach the
  core value?
- **Limited launch:** a focused early-adopter group. Test the positioning, referrals, and the
  strongest channel.
- **Broad launch:** scale the channels that earned it.
- **Metrics tied to the funnel:** acquisition (activated signups, not raw signups), retention
  (D7/D30, time to second use), and product value (a behavior showing it's part of their
  workflow).

## Traps
- Jumping straight to channels or a launch checklist.
- Defining the customer by demographics.
- Ignoring the workaround that's the real competitor.
- Defaulting to paid acquisition.
- One motion forever, with no sequencing.
- Vanity acquisition metrics (raw signups).
