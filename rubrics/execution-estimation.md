# Estimation questions (retired)

> **Category:** Analytical / Execution · **Type:** estimation / Fermi ("how many X…?"). · [All rubrics](README.md)

**In one line:** Top companies (Google, Meta, and others) have mostly dropped standalone
estimation questions because they didn't predict job performance. **Don't prep them** unless a
recruiter or job description says to. But fast, clean back-of-envelope math still comes up
*inside* other rounds, and it's a small, real signal there.

---

## What it looked like
- "How big is the US market for electric toothbrushes?"
- "How many golf balls fit in a school bus?"
- "How many Uber drivers are in the Bay Area?"

## Where the skill still shows up
- **Sizing an opportunity** in a strategy or product sense answer ("is this segment worth it?").
- **Metric tradeoffs:** quantifying a lever or a guardrail on the fly.
- **Cost math for AI products,** e.g., tokens × conversations × turns → daily inference cost (see
  [technical-ai-fluency.md](technical-ai-fluency.md)).
- **Growth equations:** writing the goal as a product of terms (see
  [strategy-growth.md](strategy-growth.md)).

## The minimum, if it does come up
State the equation first, segment the population, make round-number assumptions out loud, compute,
sanity-check against something you know, and say which assumption moves the answer most.
