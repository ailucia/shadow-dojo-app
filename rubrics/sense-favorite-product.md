# "What's your favorite product?"

> **Category:** Product Sense · **Type:** favorite product (and its variants) · [All rubrics](README.md)

**In one line:** A short, friendly warm-up (3 to 7 minutes plus follow-ups) that shows how you
think about products. Name the product, describe how its *specific design choices* fit your real
needs, and have a ready answer for "how would you change it?"

---

## Choosing the product: the three P's
- **Passion:** you genuinely care about it.
- **Perspective:** you can offer a nuanced take, beyond the obvious.
- **Personality:** it says something about you as a PM.

Popular is fine if your opinion goes past the obvious. Obscure is fine if you're ready to explain
it. It doesn't have to be the interviewing company's product. **Avoid** products too simple to
fill ten minutes (a pencil), and a direct competitor's product (telling a Lyft PM why you love
Uber).

## The framework (3 steps)
1. **Name it and explain it briefly.** More explanation if it's obscure, and call out the parts
   that matter to you.
2. **Show how it fits your needs, through design choices.** Paint who you are and what you care
   about, then connect to *specific characteristics*. "Gmail stores my email" is weak. "This
   particular UI makes organizing effortless" is strong. In the Notion example: a reading table
   linked to a notes doc per book, formats connected, in a way standalone Docs and Sheets never
   managed.
3. **Handle follow-ups.** They can go analytical ("how would you measure success?"), product ("how
   would Google build a smart version?"), or strategy ("as CEO, how would you grow it?"). **The
   most common is "how would you change it?"**, so prepare a specific pain point and a fix. In the
   Notion example: search and cross-page indexing are weak, so let users tag notes (#econ) and see
   all tagged notes together.

## Traps
- Describing what the product *does* instead of the design choices that make it good.
- Too long a description.
- No "change it" answer ready.
- Choosing something you can't talk about for ten minutes.
- A competitor's product.
